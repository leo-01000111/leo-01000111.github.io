What it does

A machine-learning pipeline that predicts the result of an upcoming Formula 1 race once qualifying is over. It is trained on seasons from 2014 onwards. Inputs are qualifying and practice results, driver and team history, circuit characteristics and the race-day forecast. An XGBoost regressor predicts every driver's finishing position, and a Plackett-Luce simulation turns those positions into probabilities for a win, P2, P3, the podium, the top 6 and the top 10. A Streamlit dashboard shows them race by race.

Prediction pipeline

01
Data ingestion. Race, qualifying, practice, pit-stop and sprint data from 2014 (FastF1), live qualifying and practice (OpenF1), and weather (Open-Meteo).
02
Features. 35 inputs: grid position and qualifying gaps, car and practice pace, driver form and Elo rating, team form, circuit history, reliability and weather. Each one uses only races before the one being predicted.
03
Model. One XGBoost regressor predicts each driver's finishing position. Recent seasons get more weight in training.
04
Race simulation. A Plackett-Luce sampler draws 10,000 finishing orders from the predicted positions, which gives a probability for every driver and every position.
05
Dashboard. A Streamlit app shows the probabilities race by race.

Model architecture

Regressor
XGBoost with an absolute-error objective, predicting finishing position (a DNF counts as last place plus one)
Sample weights
Recency weights from 0.65 for the oldest season to 1.35 for the newest
Probabilities
Plackett-Luce sampling (Gumbel-max, 10,000 draws). Each driver's row and each position's column sum to 1
Archived
An earlier stack of three XGBoost classifiers and a PyTorch MLP, combined by logistic regression, was tried and archived

Data sources

  • FastF1Race, qualifying, practice, pit-stop and sprint data from the 2014 season on
  • OpenF1Live qualifying and practice data on race weekends
  • Open-MeteoCircuit weather: temperature, rain, wind and humidity, archived and race-day forecast

Evaluation

Leave-one-season-out cross-validation, plus a holdout on the two most recent seasons, measured before the production model is retrained on almost all the data. After each real race the prediction is compared with the result. Metrics: winner accuracy, podium accuracy, P1 Brier score, Spearman correlation, mean position error and expected calibration error.

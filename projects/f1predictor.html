What it does

A machine-learning pipeline that predicts the result of an upcoming Formula 1 race once qualifying is over. It is trained on seasons from 2014 onwards. Inputs are qualifying and practice results, driver and team history, circuit characteristics and the race-day forecast. An XGBoost ranking model orders the drivers, a second model estimates each car's chance of finishing, and a race simulation turns both into probabilities for a win, P2, P3, the podium, the top 6 and the top 10. GitHub Actions runs it every race weekend and publishes the results on this page.

Prediction pipeline

01
Data ingestion. Race, qualifying, practice, pit-stop and sprint data from 2014 (FastF1), live qualifying and practice (OpenF1), and weather (Open-Meteo).
02
Features. 33 inputs: grid position and qualifying gaps, car and practice pace, driver form and Elo rating, team form, circuit history, reliability and weather. Each one uses only races before the one being predicted.
03
Model. Two parts. A ranking model (XGBoost) learns the order of the cars that reach the finish, and a logistic model gives each car's chance of finishing at all. Recent seasons get more weight in training.
04
Race simulation. 10,000 simulated races: in each, some cars retire according to their chance of finishing and the rest finish in a Plackett-Luce order drawn from the ranking scores. That gives a probability for every driver and every position.
05
Publishing. GitHub Actions predicts each race after qualifying, records the result, retrains and updates this page.

Model architecture

Two-stage model
XGBoost ranker (NDCG objective) trained on finishers only, plus a logistic model of each car's chance of finishing. Over 191 races predicted season by season (2018-2026) it beats the starting grid on log loss
Sample weights
Recency weights from 0.65 for the oldest season to 1.35 for the newest
Probabilities
Simulated races with retirements and a Plackett-Luce order (Gumbel-max, 10,000 draws), with the temperature fitted on a season the model did not train on. Each driver's row and each position's column sum to 1
Archived
Earlier models, a finishing-position regressor, a single ranker and a stack of three XGBoost classifiers plus a PyTorch MLP, were tried and archived

Data sources

  • FastF1Race, qualifying, practice, pit-stop and sprint data from the 2014 season on
  • OpenF1Live qualifying and practice data on race weekends
  • Open-MeteoCircuit weather: temperature, rain, wind and humidity, archived and race-day forecast

Evaluation

Leave-one-season-out cross-validation, plus a holdout on the two most recent seasons, measured before the production model is retrained on almost all the data. After each real race the prediction is compared with the result. Metrics: winner accuracy, podium accuracy, P1 Brier score, Spearman correlation, mean position error and expected calibration error.

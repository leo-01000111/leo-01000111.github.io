MILTOM-Bot: public-tender monitoring with a classifier trained by the sales team

In production at MILTOM. It reads Poland's public tender bulletin every hour, ranks matching tenders with a classifier, and shows them to the sales team in a web panel. Their answers train the next model.

I built this at MILTOM, a Polish manufacturer of fire-protection and drinking-water tanks. It has been in production since 16 July 2026. The code is private.

How it works

Poland publishes every public tender in the Biuletyn Zamówień Publicznych (BZP). MILTOM bids on the ones for water tanks. Before the bot, someone had to read the listings by hand every morning.

Every hour the bot fetches new notices. Keyword rules decide which ones enter the pool. A noun such as zbiornik (tank) must appear within five words of a modifier such as przeciwpożarowy (fire-protection), or the notice must carry one of four CPV codes. The admin panel edits these rules live.

The classifier sorts the pool, best tender first. Each salesperson sees one tender at a time and answers TAK (yes), NIE (no) or POMIŃ (skip, it comes back after 24 hours). A salesperson can also claim a tender so that colleagues do not work on it twice. Each answer is a training label, and the model retrains every evening.

The classifier

Featuresword TF-IDF (Polish and BZP boilerplate stopwords removed), character n-gram TF-IDF, CPV one-hot, keyword-rule signal
Modelscikit-learn logistic regression, C = 0.3
Validation5-fold stratified cross-validation, grouped by tender
Training ruleat least 30 labels, with at least 10 of each class
Fallbackkeyword matches, CPV match and TAK minus NIE votes, until a model passes the AUC threshold

On 24 September 2026 the live model was version 84, trained on 437 labels. Only 36 of them are TAK, so the 94% accuracy says little. The numbers to watch are the cross-validated AUC of 0.81, precision of 91% and recall of 68%.

Design decisions

Labels come from real work

Nobody is asked to label a dataset. The sales team decides whether a tender is worth a bid, which is already their job. The decision takes one click, and the click is the label.

Folds are grouped by tender

Several salespeople can answer the same tender. Grouping the folds by tender keeps one notice out of training and validation at the same time, so the scores are not inflated.

The model has to pass a test

A new model ranks the deck only if its cross-validated AUC is above a threshold. Otherwise the panel ranks by the simple keyword score, so a weak model never reaches the sales team.

Architecture

Six Docker Compose services on one server. All ports bind to localhost, and Caddy terminates TLS for the public sites.

ServiceRole
dbPostgreSQL 16, 17 tables, schema managed by Alembic
migrateruns the migrations once, before anything else starts
notifierAPScheduler: hourly fetch, retrain at 20:00, ops report at 21:00
adminsystem health, keyword rules, model metrics, salesperson accounts
salesmanthe tender deck, answers, claims and answer history
label_endpointsigned label links for the email digest, which is switched off since the panel replaced it

About 5,800 lines of Python: FastAPI, SQLAlchemy 2 with asyncpg, scikit-learn and selectolax. 212 tests run against a real PostgreSQL database. ruff and mypy --strict must pass.

Open work

  • Polish inflection. The keyword lists spell out every inflected form by hand. A morphological analyser such as Morfeusz2 would replace them.
  • Tender documents. The bot could read tank capacity and dimensions from the SWZ files. A first version downloaded the PDFs, but they did not fit on the 8.8 GB server disk, so I removed it.
  • LLM classification, which the sales team suggested for tenders that the keyword rules miss.

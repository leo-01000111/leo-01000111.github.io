BEng Thesis: PPO vs Robust MPC on a Simulated Roomba

Tube robust MPC and PPO compared on a simulated robot, with and without GNSS. The metrics are stability, tracking accuracy, robustness and computing cost.

Back to projects Open thesis PDF · 0.5 MB View repo

BEng thesis, Warsaw University of Technology, November 2025. Supervisor: Prof. Marcin Żugaj, DSc, Eng.

What the thesis is about

Full title: "A Comparative Analysis of Reinforcement Learning and Robust Model Predictive Control under GNSS-denial conditions using a mobile robot platform." A simulated Roomba follows a reference path to a goal through cluttered indoor rooms. It must avoid obstacles and cross a zone where GNSS is jammed. The thesis compares two controllers on stability, accuracy, robustness and computing cost. Both controllers run in the same 500 environments.

The two controllers

RMPC
Tube Robust MPC
  • Predicts future states with an explicit dynamics model. An LQR term rejects disturbances.
  • The tube bounds the effect of noise. Hard obstacle-avoidance constraints are guaranteed.
  • Conservative. It timed out in 10–15% of runs, so a real deployment needs constraint relaxation or a fallback.
PPO
Proximal Policy Optimisation (PPO)
  • Learns its policy end to end with multi-stage curriculum training. It has no explicit system model.
  • One neural-network forward pass per action. 63× faster online than RMPC.
  • Collides in 3–5% of runs. Part of this comes from a conflict between the path-following and obstacle-avoidance rewards.

P_deg-nom compares tracking error with and without denial. P_deg-InOut compares error inside and outside the denial zone. RMPC's −0.95% means its error is about the same everywhere. PPO's −75.8% means it tracks well inside the zone and loses accuracy after it leaves. This suggests that position drift builds up after denial ends.

Study setup

  • Controllers: Tube RMPC and PPO.
  • 500 evaluation environments.
  • Both controllers and both conditions use the same environments.
  • Conditions: nominal and GNSS-denied.

Tracking metric

Tracking quality is measured with cross-track error (XTE): the Euclidean distance from the robot to the nearest reference waypoint. The waypoint index can only move forward along the path.

Cost of deployment

Rollout speed is the number of actions each controller issues per second. PPO needs one neural-network forward pass per action. RMPC solves an optimisation problem at every timestep, so PPO is 63× faster.

Verdict

Safety and predictability

RMPC suits safety-critical work. It had zero collisions in 1 000 runs and guarantees its obstacle-avoidance constraints. Its weakness is a 10–15% timeout rate: the solver sometimes cannot find a feasible path in time. A real deployment would need constraint relaxation or a fallback controller.

Flexibility and online speed

PPO matched or beat RMPC on success rate and runs 63× faster online. Its error was lower inside the denial zone (0.21 m) than outside it (0.87 m). It holds the path during denial, then drifts after GNSS returns. The 3–5% collision rate comes partly from reward shaping, because path following and obstacle avoidance conflicted during training. It may not be a limit of the method.

Takeaway

Neither controller wins everywhere. Use RMPC when about 20 fps is fast enough and you need safety guarantees. PPO's adaptability and noise rejection would likely matter more in harder environments with stronger disturbances. The test environment may have been too simple for a fully fair verdict, a point raised at the defence. The thesis gives a reproducible baseline. A final comparison would need both controllers optimised to the same level.

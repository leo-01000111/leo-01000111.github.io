MostMobileMouse: a phone as a desk mouse

A phone lies face-down on the desk and works as a mouse. The rear camera tracks the ceiling, and the IMU measures the motion between frames. Work in progress.

What it does

The phone lies face-down on the desk. Slide it and the cursor moves. Tap its back to click. Twist it to scroll. Lift it to reposition, like a normal mouse.

The rear camera faces the ceiling and tracks features on it. The camera does not drift, but its data is late and depends on texture. The IMU runs at 500 Hz with low latency, but it drifts when integrated. The project combines the two. Development is data-first: record real sensor data, build the algorithm offline in Python, measure it, and only then write the real-time app.

How it works

Recorder app
Kotlin and Camera2 on a Galaxy S24. 640×480 grey frames at 60 fps, 2 ms manual exposure, OIS and EIS off, gyro and accelerometer at 500 Hz. Camera and gyro clocks agree to 2 ms (correlation 0.9997).
Vision front end
Lucas–Kanade tracks with a gyro-predicted start, a forward–backward check, and gyro de-rotation. RANSAC then fits the phone's translation.
Multiple depths
A real desk has shelves and a monitor in view, and only 45 % of points fit one plane. The phone moves in a plane, so all static points flow in parallel. Each track gets its own inverse depth.
Scale, clicks, lift
The accelerometer measures each stroke between two stops and sets the scale. A jerk spike is a tap. A tilt above 4° is a lift and freezes the cursor.

Current prototype

The phone streams frames and IMU data to the PC over USB, or over Wi-Fi as JPEG. A Python engine computes the motion and moves the Windows cursor. A frame reaches the PC 51 ms (median) after mid-exposure. A small aim-lab game compares the phone with a real mouse and auto-tunes the settings.

Next steps

  • An EKF that fuses the IMU and the per-track vision measurements, with zero-velocity updates. Then a go/no-go check against accuracy targets.
  • A C++ core (OpenCV, Eigen) that runs on the phone. Only cursor events then cross the link. The raw stream needs about 150 Mbit/s.
  • Bluetooth HID, so the phone pairs as a normal mouse with no PC software. The S24 supports the HID device profile.

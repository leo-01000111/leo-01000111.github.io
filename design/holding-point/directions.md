# Directions explored

Four directions were drawn up before choosing. Each is a complete vibe on its own; the brief was *a clear identity, Bauhaus-adjacent but not Bauhaus, and specific to Leon* — an aerospace engineer moving into robotics, Polish, working across PL/EN/FR, who also teaches. The live comparison is the **Directions** card under Explorations.

## A — Unism

Pure Polish avant-garde, after Władysław Strzemiński and Katarzyna Kobro. The page is one unified field with no focal hero; rhythm comes from Kobro's 8:5 ratio; colour is vermilion, ultramarine and black on raw linen; shapes are interlocking planes that run off the frame.

- **For:** deeply specific to a Polish designer's heritage; mathematically generated, which suits an engineer.
- **Against:** the palette and planes read as "Bauhaus" to most visitors, the thing you asked not to copy. It is a museum mood — it says *art history*, not *engineering*. It has no built-in answer for navigation or status.
- **Kept:** the 8:5 proportion system, the Fibonacci spacing scale, and the one off-frame bleed per page.

## B — Step Response

Control theory as the aesthetic. Log-decade spacing (1, 2, 5, 10…), Bode-plot hairlines as the grid, phosphor-on-black signal traces, every number with its unit.

- **For:** it *is* your field, and nobody else's portfolio looks like a Bode plot.
- **Against:** oscilloscope-green-on-black is the most common "engineer" cliché after the blueprint, it is dark-only by nature, and it would push the tutoring page (parents and teenagers) into something cold and hard to read.
- **Kept:** the motion model — every transition is a lightly damped step response.

## C — Title Block

An evolution of the current site: an ISO drawing sheet with a title block footer, revision triangles, blue-line grid, dimension-line dividers.

- **For:** safe, consistent with the CAD work at Miltom, cheap to migrate.
- **Against:** it is what the site already is. Blueprint-dark with an indigo accent is also the default "engineering portfolio" look, so it does not give a clear new identity.
- **Kept:** the discipline of the DataPlate (the spec block survives, recast as a riveted data plate).

## D — Holding Point ✓ chosen

Airfield ground signage as the component language: black location plates with yellow legends, yellow direction signs with arrows, red mandatory signs, blue edge lights, green centreline lights, pavement markings. Composed with direction A's 8:5 constructivist layout and direction B's step-response motion.

- **For:** it comes straight out of aviation, but from the *ground* side most people never look at, so it is recognisable without being a cliché. Every colour and shape has a fixed, learnable meaning, which makes navigation self-explanatory (the nav literally says where you are and points to everything else). It shares Bauhaus's flat colour and hard geometry — the part you like — but for functional reasons, not stylistic ones. It works in a bright Day theme and a Night theme, and it is friendly enough for the tutoring page.
- **Risks, and how the system handles them:** yellow everywhere would be loud → a ≤10% yellow budget and "never text on the light ground". Signage can look like a theme park → no pictograms, no fake airport codes, no runway numbers as decoration; the signs only carry the site's real destinations.
